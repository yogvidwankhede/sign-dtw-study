\section{Ethics and broader impact}
\label{sec:ethics}
\paragraph{Who did the work.} This study was designed and run by hearing researchers without a Deaf co-author. Deaf-led
analysis of sign language AI argues that such work often centres hearing researchers' priorities and a ``communication
barrier'' framing \citep{desai2024biases}. We chose a methods comparison on an existing, consented dataset rather than an
application, and we make no claim that any result benefits Deaf people.

\paragraph{Data.} PopSign participants consented to inclusion in a public database, and the dataset card lists identification
and appearance-based uses as out of scope; we keep face landmarks local, release only hand and pose landmarks, and never
attempt to identify signers. The dataset card also discourages continuous-signing and translation claims, which we avoid.

\paragraph{Risks.} A cheap recogniser could be presented as a substitute for interpreters or language access. High accuracy
on 250 isolated signs does not support that, and we discourage it. Community perspectives on automatic sign language
translation \citep{tran2023assets} and dataset governance \citep{bragg2021fate} should be consulted before any deployment.
