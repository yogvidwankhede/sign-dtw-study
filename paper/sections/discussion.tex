\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the data say.} On PopSign with signer-independent splits and hand-angle features, small learned models beat
nearest-neighbour DTW from one example per sign upward, at vocabularies of 10 and 50 signs, and the two pre-registered
expectations came out as: H1 (DTW ahead with few examples) not supported, H2 (learned models ahead with all data) supported. With
one example per sign and 50 signs every method is weak (the best point estimate is \accTransVFiftyKOne\%). Euclidean
nearest neighbour was never worse than DTW at 50 signs, and DBA prototypes were poor. Preprocessing mattered a great deal for the
template-matching baselines (\tuneGapDtw{} points on validation between the naive and the chosen setting) and little for the learned
models, which is a reason to tune baselines before concluding anything about them.

\paragraph{Why DTW trails is untested.} Several explanations are plausible and we did not test them: the per-frame angle features are
noisy, so extra alignment freedom may bring unrelated signs closer; the learned models can weight informative dimensions while DTW
weights all dimensions equally; and signer variation, which a model trained on 31 signers can absorb, is a direct cost for
template matching. Adding hand location and the preprocessing choices narrowed the gap only partly. We therefore make no causal
claim about the mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to other results.} In general time-series classification, tuned nearest-neighbour DTW is a strong baseline
\citep{bagnall2014nn}. Our result does not contradict that finding, because the setting differs: 420-dimensional noisy features
from landmark detection, 50 classes, and test signers unseen in training; we did not investigate how far each of these differences
matters. The earlier conference paper from the same author \citep{mankar2024} reported DTW ahead of an LSTM on a small custom
dataset without a signer-independent split; we cannot re-run that experiment, and the present signer-independent results do not
support carrying its conclusion over to this setting.

\paragraph{Practical reading, with limits.} For this dataset and these features, a small learned model is the better default once
a few labelled examples per sign and a few seconds of training are available, and it is also much cheaper at inference when many
reference videos exist. Template matching keeps its practical advantages of requiring no training and of letting a new sign be added
by storing one example, but in our experiments that convenience cost accuracy. This is a statement about one dataset, one language
and our feature choices (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); it says nothing about whether any of these systems is useful to Deaf people,
and accuracy on isolated signs is not communication access (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}).
