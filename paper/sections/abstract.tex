\begin{abstract}
We ask when matching a sign's hand landmarks against stored examples with dynamic time warping (DTW) is competitive with trained
neural networks for isolated sign recognition. On PopSign ASL v1.0 (American Sign Language, smartphone video) with the dataset's
signer-disjoint split, we compare DTW and Euclidean nearest neighbour, DTW prototypes, a 1D-CNN, a BiGRU and a small Transformer on the
same MediaPipe features, varying the examples per sign from 1 to all and the vocabulary from 10 to \nVocabMax{} signs.
The analysis was pre-registered, each method family chose its own preprocessing on validation signers, and intervals come from
resampling the \nTestSigners{} test signers. Contrary to our pre-registered expectation, the learned models beat DTW at every number of
examples per sign we tried: at 50 signs and 10 examples per sign the CNN scores \accCnnVFiftyKTen\% top-1 against \accDtwVFiftyKTen\% for DTW,
and with all training data \accTransVFiftyKAll\% against \accDtwVFiftyKAll\%. A second, pre-registered replication on ASL Citizen
(10 to 300 signs, \cznSignersTest{} test participants) supports both hypotheses again, and Euclidean nearest neighbour is at least as good as DTW in most cells
on both datasets. Exploratory extensions (MiniRocket, soft-DTW prototypes, longer training, per-$k$ tuning, richer features) raise some baselines, DTW to at best
\ablBestDtwKAll\% with all PopSign data, but do not close the gap; an embedding trained on other signs is best with few examples per sign on both datasets. We report latency, training cost, ablations and limitations. This is an isolated-sign
study of one sign language on two datasets; it is not translation and makes no claim about benefit to Deaf users.
\end{abstract}
