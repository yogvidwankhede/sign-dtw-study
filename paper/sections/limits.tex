\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One sign language, two datasets.} All results are for ASL on PopSign (47 signers, selfie phone video, mostly one-handed
signs) and ASL Citizen (crowd-sourced webcam video). We do not test other languages, cameras or continuous signing.
\item \textbf{Few signers.} The PopSign test split has 8 signers and ASL Citizen 11 participants, so signer-clustered intervals are wide and small
differences are not resolvable. Training noise (3 seeds) is smaller than signer variation.
\item \textbf{Vocabulary range.} The vocabulary sweep reaches 300 signs only on ASL Citizen; on PopSign it reaches 100 signs (the 100-sign condition uses settings tuned at 50 signs and an independent query draw).
\item \textbf{Small models, limited tuning.} The learned models are small and tuned on a small grid at one condition
(50 signs, 10 examples per sign). All three chose the longest epoch budget in the grid. Longer training and per-$k$ tuning changed little (Section~\ref{sec:extension}), but larger models
or pre-training may change the picture.
\item \textbf{Only F1 in the main grid, and a handicap risk for DTW.} The main comparison uses the hand-angle features of the
earlier implementation, which carry no hand location or orientation. DTW and Euclidean matching each chose among the preprocessing
options listed in Section~\ref{sec:methods} on validation signers, and richer features helped both families (Table~\ref{tab:abl}), but other options, local costs or
learned metrics might favour template matching more.
\item \textbf{Baselines not run.} We ran one learned-embedding baseline (trained on other signs; cf. \citealp{vandendriessche2025onesh}) but not the full set of time-series classifiers in
the bake-off \citep{bagnall2017bakeoff}, nor large pre-trained or end-to-end video models.
\item \textbf{Landmark quality.} Results depend on MediaPipe 0.10.14 and on the resize used; the fraction of videos or frames
without detected hands is reported. Face and other non-manual signals are mostly not modelled.
\item \textbf{Not translation, not an application.} Top-1 accuracy on isolated signs is not communication access. We did not
evaluate any system with Deaf users.
\item \textbf{No phonological analysis.} Neither dataset has phonological annotation; the descriptive analyses make no claim about phonological similarity.
\item \textbf{Timing.} Latency was measured on one Apple M4 Max laptop with other processes running; no low-power device was
available, so embedded performance is not measured.
\item \textbf{Pre-registration scope.} The PopSign plan and the ASL Citizen replication were written by the author before the respective test scoring, but are not registered reports and
were not independently time-stamped; the git history is the record (and it was rewritten once to remove cache files, see \texttt{PREREG.md}). The extensions are post-hoc.
\item \textbf{ASL Citizen reproducibility.} Its licence forbids redistributing the data or derived landmarks, so the replication can be re-run only by fetching the data again; we release aggregate results only.
\end{itemize}
